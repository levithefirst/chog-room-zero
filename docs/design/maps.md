# Locations

Owner 2026-10-18 (IDENTITY + MAPS): keep Room Zero, add Office and Supermarket, open to everyone, chosen before the
match. Owner 2026-10-19 (H34), after playing them: "I just played the office and supermarket, no difference????" and
"exactly five initial locations ... each map must create a substantially different tactical experience ... The
difference must exist in the actual collision/topology, not merely visuals." H33's Office and Supermarket were the
same 64 x 40 shell (a spawn strip at each end, three east-west bands, a contested middle) with different furniture:
that failure is the brief for this document.

Code:
- `src/config/maps.ts` is the registry.
- `src/config/layouts.ts` is generated by `tools/maps/build.py`, which draws the west half from rectangles, mirrors it and checks it.
- `src/sim/level.ts` holds the tiles. `src/sim/vision.ts` holds the lit areas. `src/render/bake.ts` draws both.
- Tests: `tests/h34-maps.test.ts` (rules and identity) and `tests/id-maps.test.ts` (every location playable, solo and online).

## Why they used to feel the same, and what changed

Two causes, both measured:

1. **One shape.** Same size, same spawn strips, same three bands. Fixed by redesigning each from how the real place is
   organised (research below) and by giving each its own size and topology.
2. **One fight distance.** Every operator sees a 7.5-12 m lamp cone in the dark, so geometry beyond ~9 m never
   mattered. Measured over 8 autopilot matches per location (H33-style dark everywhere): the median hit was 4.3-5.3 m
   on every map. Fixed by **lit areas** (below). Afterwards: Supermarket median 6.2 m, p90 9.9 m; Metro 6.3 / 8.9;
   Room Zero unchanged at 4.5 / 6.7.

New rules the maps use (all in the shared sim, so the bots, the server and the screen agree):

| Tile / rule | Does | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Rail `r` | Blocks bodies; never sight, bullets or grenades. Not cover. | Railings, track beds, open window frames, the glass boardroom. |
| Deck `u` | Raised floor. A shot fired from a deck clears every crate and barrier below. No cover on it but walls. | Scaffolding, the first-floor slab. |
| Stairs `s` | Floor at 65% speed (`TUNING.movement.stairsMul`). The only way onto a deck. | Stair towers, station stairs, track crossings. |
| Lit area | A body standing in one is seen from 22 m (`TUNING.vision.litRange`) inside your cone with a clear line. | Store floor, platform, open plan, floodlights. |
| Footstep surface | Carpet 0.7x, deck / stairs 1.3x, service tunnel 1.3x (`TUNING.sound.surface`). | Office carpet, metal decks, the Metro tunnel. |

A deck can only be reached by stairs: every deck tile touches only deck, stairs, rails or walls (checked by the
builder and the tests).

## Research the layouts come from

- **Office:** open workspace, enclosed meeting rooms, private offices, reception, support rooms, a building core
  (lifts, risers, toilets) and the circulation corridors between them.
- **Supermarket:** entrance and exit in the front corners, the checkout bank across the front, parallel gondola
  aisles (a grid), perimeter fresh departments, the refrigerated back wall, and back-of-house (stockroom, receiving)
  with its own restocking flow.
- **Metro station:** street stairs into a ticket hall, a fare-control line (banks of gates), a paid concourse or
  mezzanine, stairs down to an island or side platforms, the track bed, and staff rooms and service passages beside
  it.
  - Sources: transit-enthusiast station surveys (subwaynut.com) covering mezzanine fare lines and island vs side platforms.
- **Construction site:** a building around a central stair and lift core, a slab with a column grid, perimeter
  scaffolding, and laydown and staging yards near the footprint.
  - Sources: university construction updates (BU Center for Computing & Data Sciences) on the stair core and the hoist in the core.
  - Also a Penn State AE thesis on site logistics (laydown in one corner, materials near the footprint).
- **Level design:** the three-lane rule, every lane with its own purpose. Sources: The Level Design Book on circulation, and the Treyarch interview on "every lane needs a purpose".

### The differentiation questions

**Room Zero: balanced baseline** (kept as is, all dark).

1. **Range:** short, lamp length.
2. **Choke points:** the doors into the side rooms.
3. **Defensive positions:** the corner offices and storage.
4. **Flanks:** the north-south flank corridors.
5. **Longest sightline:** the dock, 28 m.
6. **Close quarters:** the side rooms.
7. **Bots:** patrol across two or three rooms.
8. **Rotation:** gallery, corridors, atrium and dock.
9. **Pickups:** spread out.
10. **Danger zone:** ends in the atrium or the dock.
11. **Powers:** all of them, about equally.
12. **Unique decision:** none. It is the easiest map to learn.

**Office: room clearing, corridors, mid range.** An office floor around a solid core: the centre is an obstacle you
fight around, not an arena.

1. **Range:** short to mid. Mid across the lit open plan, short everywhere else.
2. **Choke points:**
   - the single doors of 16 private offices and 3 meeting rooms;
   - the two boardroom doors;
   - the ring corridor's doors.
3. **Defensive positions:** the meeting rooms (one door each) and the boardroom (glass on two sides, so it is seen into).
4. **Flanks:** the dark service corridor behind the server room and kitchen (south), and the north corridor.
5. **Longest sightline:** the north corridor, broken by a fire door.
6. **Close quarters:** the private offices, the core toilets and the server racks.
7. **Bots:** door to door. The Board bot holds the boardroom, the Server bot the south lane.
8. **Rotation:** around the core, either way.
9. **Pickups:** health and armour in rooms you have to enter (offices, meeting rooms, server room); ammo in the corridors.
10. **Danger zone:** ends in reception or the kitchen, so the fight is pulled through doors.
11. **Powers (design expectation, not measured):**
    - Visor is worth most here: rooms and doors everywhere hide bodies.
    - Drool Web always has a wall in reach.
    - X-Ray (DEMO only) would be strongest here.
12. **Unique decision:** clear the room or pass the door. The carpet muffles your steps to 70%, so a careful creep works here and nowhere else.

**Supermarket: long aisles, shelf cover, directional sightlines.** One bright hall whose aisles run north-south,
across the line between the sides.

1. **Range:** the longest fights. The sales floor is lit, so a body down an aisle is seen from its end (22 m).
2. **Choke points:** every aisle mouth on the action alley and the back aisle (each is a crossing in the open), and the two swing doors into the stockroom.
3. **Defensive positions:** the end caps (crates at the aisle mouths) and the checkout comb (counters you shoot over).
4. **Flanks:** the dark stockroom loop behind the back wall: the only way round unseen.
5. **Longest sightline:** the power aisle and the front and back aisles, 70 m (columns break the end-to-end shot).
6. **Close quarters:** the stockroom racks and the produce and deli bins.
7. **Bots:** they hold aisles and patrol the alleys. Their sight reaches as far as yours in the light.
8. **Rotation:** only three ways between the halves: the stockroom, the back aisle and the front. The power aisle joins front and back, not the halves.
9. **Pickups:** ammo in the aisles (you have to step into a lane), armour in the stockroom and the checkouts.
10. **Danger zone:** ends in the power aisle, the alley, the back aisle or the dock. A power-aisle ending forces the longest fight.
11. **Powers (design expectation):**
    - Laser Eyes down an aisle.
    - Aura to cross an aisle mouth.
    - Bubble on whoever is caught in a lane.
12. **Unique decision:** cross the next aisle mouth, wait at the end cap, or go round through the stockroom. Office has nothing like it: its lanes are corridors you walk along; here every lane cuts across your path.

**Metro Station: choke points, ambushes, platform and tunnels.**

1. **Range:** two ranges at once. The lit concourse and platform are long (70 m lanes, pillars), while the carriages, rooms and tunnel are knife fights in the dark.
2. **Choke points:**
   - the fare-gate line (four one-wide gates);
   - the stair wells (slow, loud);
   - the two track crossings (stairs in the open, under the platform's eyes).
3. **Defensive positions:** the top of the stairs, the platform shelter and the carriage doors.
4. **Flanks:** the dark service tunnel from each spawn to the middle rooms. It doglegs and echoes (1.3x), so ambushes are heard coming.
5. **Longest sightline:** the platform, 70 m.
6. **Close quarters:** the train carriages (clear them car by car) and the tunnel.
7. **Bots:** they climb the stairs, hold carriages and walk the tunnel. Tracks are rails, so they path round them.
8. **Rotation:** concourse, or tunnel, then up or down through stairs. Never across a track except at a crossing.
9. **Pickups:**
   - the platform's health is out in the open;
   - armour sits in the concourse and tunnel;
   - grenades are in the tunnel and rooms.
10. **Danger zone:** ends mid-platform or on the concourse, so it drags everyone up the stairs.
11. **Powers (design expectation):**
    - Bubble at a gate or stair.
    - Visor in the tunnel.
    - Laser Eyes along the platform.
12. **Unique decision:** take the platform (seen from afar), the tunnel (unseen but close) or the concourse (gates). Every change of level is a slow stair.

**Construction Site: high ground, exposure, long sightlines, partial cover.**

1. **Range:** long and open (mean open sight 10.8 m, the most), with little cover (0.02 per tile, the least).
2. **Choke points:** the stair towers at the scaffold ends and the core stairs onto the slab.
3. **Defensive positions:**
   - the slab, which is high ground: shots from it clear the crates below;
   - its four columns, the only cover up there.
4. **Flanks:** the dark ground floor under the scaffolds (columns and half-built walls), and the yards.
5. **Longest sightline:** the lit strips outside the scaffolds, 66 m.
6. **Close quarters:** the core rooms and the cabin and tool store.
7. **Bots:** the Slab bot holds the high ground and the Scaffold bot walks the scaffold. They use the stairs (tested).
8. **Rotation:**
   - outside: fast, lit and exposed;
   - through the building: slow, dark and covered;
   - over the scaffold: high, loud, and you shoot down through window frames.
9. **Pickups:** armour and health on the slab (high ground pays), ammo on the scaffolds.
10. **Danger zone:** ends on the slab or the core stairs. Whoever holds the slab holds the end.
11. **Powers (design expectation):**
    - Aura to climb the stairs under fire.
    - Laser Eyes from the slab.
    - Drool Web is weak on the slab (no wall near).
12. **Unique decision:** take the exposed fast route or the slow covered one, and whether to climb. High ground beats cover, but you are lit and loud getting there.

## Shared rules (all locations)

- **Pickups** come back 4 s after they are taken, chosen by the server:
  - never on top of a player;
  - at least `TUNING.pickups.minSeparation` apart;
  - kept even between the two halves.
- **The danger zone** runs the same schedule (`docs/design/zone.md`) with each location's own last centres. All of them are on open floor, and at least one is fair to both online sides (tested).
- **Online:** players are matched only with players who chose the same location and mode. Protocol 2 (H34): an older client is told to reload.
